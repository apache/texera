This section showcases example workflows, use cases, and demonstrations to help you understand Texera in action.
{{% /pageinfo %}}

Texera makes it easy to design and execute data analytics workflows visually.

---

## 🧩 Example Workflows

Step-by-step examples you can follow today:

- **[Filter and aggregate a CSV file](/docs/tutorials/create-dataset-upload-data/)**: upload data, keep the rows you need, and compute averages.
- **[Add a column with Python](/docs/tutorials/guide-to-use-python-udf/)**: write a small Python UDF, and see what common errors mean.
- **[Bring a Jupyter notebook into Texera](/docs/tutorials/migrate-jupyter-notebook/)**: turn notebook cells into a workflow.

Pages for text analytics, joins, machine learning and visualization examples are planned. Until then, see the [operator reference](/docs/reference/operators/).

---

## 💡 Ready-made examples

- **Your own Texera ([Docker install](/docs/getting-started/installing-using-docker/))**: start it with `docker compose --profile examples up` to pre-create two example workflows and their datasets.
- **Any Texera**: open **Hub → Workflows** to browse workflows others have made public.
- **Import a workflow file**: on **Your Work → Workflows**, click the cloud upload icon next to **Create Workflow** and choose a workflow `.json` or `.zip` file.
